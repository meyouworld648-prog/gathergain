Simple Ways to Make Home Feel More Natural

The feeling of being outdoors can influence the way a home feels too. Natural materials, plants, daylight, and simple textures can bring some of that connection indoors.

Make More of Natural Light

Natural light can change the atmosphere of a room throughout the day. Where possible, keep windows clear and arrange furniture so that useful daylight can reach the areas where you spend the most time.

Notice how the light changes during the day rather than treating every room as though it has the same conditions.

Add Natural Materials

Wood, stone, woven fibres, cotton, linen, and other natural-looking textures can add warmth and variation to a room.

You do not need to replace everything. A few carefully chosen details can be enough to introduce a more natural feeling.

Bring in Plants

Plants are an obvious connection to the outdoors, but they should suit the space they are placed in. Consider available light, temperature, and the amount of care you realistically want to provide.

  • Choose plants that suit the available light.
  • Use different shapes and textures where appropriate.
  • Keep plants where they can be comfortably maintained.
  • Use simple containers that complement the room.
  • Allow some areas to remain uncluttered.

Want to bring a little more of the outdoors into everyday life? Explore more ideas inspired by natural spaces.

Bring More Nature Into Your Day

Use Texture Instead of Clutter

A natural feeling does not require a room to be filled with decorative objects. A textured throw, wooden surface, woven basket, or simple plant can add interest without making the space feel crowded.

Keep the Outdoors in Mind

Views of gardens, trees, skies, or other outdoor surroundings can become part of the character of a room. Arrange seating or everyday spaces so you can enjoy those views where possible.

Create a Calmer Connection

The aim is not to turn your home into an outdoor space. It is simply to introduce some of the qualities you appreciate in nature: light, texture, greenery, openness, and variation.

Small changes can make those qualities part of everyday surroundings without requiring a major redesign.
